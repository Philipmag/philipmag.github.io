# Digital Guardians: Senior Facilitators
## Six Student Employee Job Descriptions

> **ScaffoldStudioCo** | Working operational document prepared for **Digital Guardians** and the proposed Trent University micro-credential.

**Document status:** Working draft for program development and institutional review  
**Program:** *Digital Guardians: Cybersecurity and Digital Inclusion Facilitators*  
**Planned delivery:** Eight weeks beginning October 6, 2026, subject to academic and community-partner approval  
**Source basis:** *Digital Guardians Micro-Credential Proposal* supplied by the program team.

## Purpose and common operating context

Digital Guardians is a proposed community-engaged micro-credential that prepares 50 to 60 student participants to help older adults use technology safely, confidently, independently, and with dignity. The participant program combines cybersecurity, digital inclusion, video communication, introductory artificial-intelligence literacy, accessible communication, research, reflection, and supervised community practice. Participants work in teams of four to six and may be assigned to partners such as Activity Haven, Peterborough Public Library, the New Canadians Centre, or another approved organization that serves older adults.

The six positions below are **student employee** roles. Together, the Senior Facilitators train, mentor, coordinate, observe, and support the 50 to 60 program participants. They do not replace the approved academic lead or designated assessor, who retains responsibility for the final micro-credential decision. The Senior Facilitators provide structured feedback, maintain operational records, and contribute evidence for assessment through approved processes.

All six employees work as one facilitation team. Each employee carries a specialist portfolio while also serving as a cohort mentor for approximately 8 to 12 participants and providing cross-coverage when another facilitator is unavailable. Participant workload remains six hours per week for eight weeks, plus a two-hour onboarding session: 1.5 hours of online modules and knowledge checks, 1.5 hours of applied practice and workshop preparation, one hour of community workshop participation or facilitation, one hour of research, reflection, and portfolio work, and one hour of team debrief, feedback review, and questions.

## Shared standards for every Senior Facilitator

Every Senior Facilitator is expected to communicate without jargon, shame, or age-based assumptions. They must use plain language, invite questions, protect privacy, and respect that older adults bring valuable experience and different levels of digital confidence. They must not ask participants or community members to reveal passwords, verification codes, banking credentials, or other sensitive personal information.

Every Senior Facilitator must complete required orientation and follow approved procedures for accessibility, confidentiality, records management, community-partner conduct, health and safety, and escalation. If a participant or older adult faces an apparent fraud, account-security, safety, accessibility, or welfare concern, the facilitator must pause the activity where appropriate and escalate it to the Program Coordinator or Academic Lead according to the approved process. Senior Facilitators must never present themselves as financial, legal, medical, or law-enforcement professionals.

## Reporting structure and role boundaries

Each Senior Facilitator reports to the **Program Coordinator / Academic Lead**, or to another designated supervisor confirmed through Trent University approval. The Community Workshop Operations Lead maintains day-to-day communication with approved partner contacts, but does not make commitments on behalf of the university or the partner without supervisor approval.

The approved Academic Lead or designated assessor makes the final decision on completion and badge eligibility. The Senior Facilitators may prepare feedback, document attendance, complete observation records, identify missing evidence, and recommend supports. They may not independently award, deny, or alter a participant’s academic result.

# Position 1 — Senior Facilitator, Learning and Curriculum Lead

## Position summary

The Senior Facilitator, Learning and Curriculum Lead leads the instructional spine of the eight-week program. This employee converts approved curriculum content into clear, age-friendly demonstrations and practice activities that prepare program participants to facilitate safely with older adults. The role ensures that every participant receives consistent preparation across cybersecurity, digital inclusion, privacy, video communication, and introductory responsible artificial intelligence.

## Key responsibilities and duties

The Learning and Curriculum Lead prepares the weekly learning plan, slides, demonstrations, scenario cards, and practice activities before each instructional session. The employee confirms that the materials align with the program sequence: digital inclusion and online risks in Weeks 1–2; passwords, multi-factor authentication, privacy, safe browsing, software updates, and account/device security in Weeks 3–4; video communication and introductory AI literacy in Weeks 5–6; and supervised workshop delivery, feedback use, reflection, and portfolio completion in Weeks 7–8.

The employee models how to explain technical ideas in plain language. For example, they demonstrate how to turn a technical term such as “phishing” into a short description, a realistic example, safe next steps, and a check-for-understanding question. They facilitate rehearsal activities in which participants practise explaining a digital-safety issue to a peer acting as an older adult with limited digital confidence.

The employee coordinates with the Digital Safety and Current-Threat Lead to ensure that examples are current, credible, accurate, and suitable for an older-adult audience. They coordinate with the Accessibility, Technology and Resources Lead to ensure that learning materials can be used in accessible formats and that demonstrations work on available devices.

Each week, the employee reviews questions that emerged from modules, practice sessions, workshops, reflections, and debriefs. They identify recurring misunderstandings and revise the next session’s examples or prompts so that the facilitation team responds consistently. They also provide instructional support to the other five Senior Facilitators when they deliver small-group or make-up learning sessions.

Success in this role is demonstrated when participants can explain core concepts accurately, use plain-language teaching methods, and enter community workshops with a clear, approved plan rather than relying on unsupported improvisation.

## Qualifications and requirements

The successful candidate is a currently enrolled Trent University student, subject to the employment requirements in effect at appointment. They demonstrate strong written and oral communication, sound organization, and an interest in cybersecurity, digital inclusion, adult learning, community engagement, education, social services, library work, or related fields.

The candidate can explain technology without unnecessary jargon and is comfortable presenting to small and large groups. They can work with structured teaching materials, incorporate feedback, and adjust an explanation when a learner does not understand it. Experience tutoring, teaching, supporting community programs, working with older adults, or facilitating peer learning is an asset.

The candidate must complete required program training and any site-specific orientation. They must follow accessibility, privacy, and respectful-conduct requirements.

## Reporting relationships

The Learning and Curriculum Lead reports to the Program Coordinator / Academic Lead. The role works closely with the Assessment and Feedback Lead, Digital Safety and Current-Threat Lead, Accessibility, Technology and Resources Lead, and all cohort mentors. The position has no independent authority to make final academic assessment decisions.

# Position 2 — Senior Facilitator, Community Workshop Operations Lead

## Position summary

The Senior Facilitator, Community Workshop Operations Lead coordinates the practical delivery of supervised community workshops. This employee makes sure that participant teams arrive prepared, the learning environment is organized, approved partners have the information they need, and Senior Facilitators can observe and support workshop delivery without interrupting the dignity or independence of older adult participants.

## Key responsibilities and duties

The Community Workshop Operations Lead develops and maintains the operational schedule for workshop preparation, partner-site sessions, room set-up, team assignments, materials transport, arrival times, accessibility needs communicated through approved channels, and debriefs. The employee helps match 10 to 15 participant teams of four to six students with approved community opportunities, based on the direction of the Program Coordinator / Academic Lead and community-partner capacity.

Before a workshop, the employee confirms the approved agenda, expected attendance, room layout, device availability, connectivity, accessible-format needs, site contact, emergency procedures, and the facilitation coverage plan. They ensure that every participant team has an approved workshop plan, teaching materials, and a named Senior Facilitator observer or support contact.

During a workshop, the employee serves as the operations point person. They coordinate arrival and check-in, distribute materials, address routine logistical disruptions, and direct concerns to the correct Senior Facilitator or supervisor. They make sure that no participant team is left to facilitate alone when observation or support is required. They also protect the workshop’s age-friendly purpose by ensuring adequate time for introductions, questions, repetition, and hands-on practice.

After a workshop, the employee gathers approved attendance and site-confirmation records, notes logistical improvements, and leads the transfer of non-academic feedback to the Assessment and Feedback Lead and the Program Coordinator / Academic Lead. They do not collect unnecessary personal information about older adult attendees.

Success in this role is demonstrated when community workshops are prepared, respectful, accessible, staffed, and consistently documented; when participant teams know their responsibilities; and when partner-site concerns are handled promptly through the appropriate supervisor.

## Qualifications and requirements

The successful candidate is a currently enrolled Trent University student, subject to employment requirements in effect at appointment. They demonstrate strong organization, reliable follow-through, clear communication, and the ability to manage schedules, checklists, and multiple small teams.

The candidate is comfortable communicating professionally with community organizations and can remain calm when plans change. Experience organizing events, coordinating volunteers, supporting workshops, working in community programs, or providing customer or client service is an asset.

The candidate must complete required program, community-partner, health-and-safety, privacy, accessibility, and site-specific training. A police record check or other screening may be required only where an approved host site or university policy requires it.

## Reporting relationships

The Community Workshop Operations Lead reports to the Program Coordinator / Academic Lead. The role is the designated operational liaison to approved host sites under supervisor direction. It works closely with all other Senior Facilitators, particularly the Accessibility, Technology and Resources Lead and the Assessment and Feedback Lead.

# Position 3 — Senior Facilitator, Assessment and Feedback Lead

## Position summary

The Senior Facilitator, Assessment and Feedback Lead organizes the evidence and feedback process that supports the competency-based micro-credential. This employee helps participants understand what they must demonstrate, monitors completion evidence, provides timely formative feedback through approved rubrics and templates, and alerts the Academic Lead when a participant may be at risk of missing a requirement.

## Key responsibilities and duties

The Assessment and Feedback Lead maintains the approved assessment tracker for online modules and knowledge checks, four current-threat or emerging-technology briefs, eight weekly reflections, required workshop plans and accessible teaching materials, workshop attendance and participation evidence, observed facilitation records, end-of-program reflection, and capstone digital portfolio. The tracker must distinguish between evidence received, feedback provided, resubmission or support needed, and final academic decision. It must not identify a participant as badge-complete until the approved Academic Lead or designated assessor has made that decision.

The employee explains competency-based assessment in practical terms. Participants must show that they understand a topic and can apply it safely, clearly, and respectfully with older adults; attendance alone is not sufficient. The employee uses approved criteria to help participants identify strengths, correct safety-critical gaps, and plan a specific next step.

The employee coordinates feedback cycles with the six-person facilitation team. They review whether feedback is consistent across teams, make sure that comments are respectful and actionable, and route academic or accommodation questions to the Academic Lead. They maintain a confidential list of missing or incomplete evidence and prepare weekly status summaries for the Program Coordinator / Academic Lead.

The employee assists with the live-facilitation observation process by assigning approved observers, ensuring observation records are complete, and confirming that debrief feedback is returned to the relevant participant team. They protect participant privacy and do not discuss individual performance in open group settings.

Success in this role is demonstrated when each participant understands the required evidence, receives prompt and consistent feedback, and can identify what they must do to demonstrate competency before the end of Week 8.

## Qualifications and requirements

The successful candidate is a currently enrolled Trent University student, subject to the employment requirements in effect at appointment. They demonstrate careful organization, sound judgment, discretion, strong written communication, and the ability to use rubrics, checklists, or learning-management tools consistently.

The candidate can give feedback that is specific, respectful, and focused on observable work. They understand the difference between formative feedback and a final academic decision. Experience as a teaching assistant, tutor, peer mentor, grader, facilitator, or program administrator is an asset.

The candidate must complete training on confidentiality, accessibility, approved assessment practices, records handling, and escalation. They must not make independent final grading or badge-award decisions.

## Reporting relationships

The Assessment and Feedback Lead reports to the Program Coordinator / Academic Lead and works most closely with the Academic Lead or designated assessor. The role coordinates evidence collection across all six Senior Facilitators, but final assessment authority remains with the approved Academic Lead or designated assessor.

# Position 4 — Senior Facilitator, Student Mentorship and Team Support Lead

## Position summary

The Senior Facilitator, Student Mentorship and Team Support Lead builds the day-to-day support system for the 50 to 60 program participants. This employee ensures that teams of four to six students understand their roles, receive timely coaching, use feedback constructively, and can seek help before a small problem becomes a barrier to safe community facilitation.

## Key responsibilities and duties

The Student Mentorship and Team Support Lead establishes the participant-team check-in process during onboarding. They help each team agree on a communication routine, task allocation, meeting expectations, respectful group norms, and a method for raising concerns. They maintain an assigned cohort of approximately 8 to 12 participants while coordinating with the other Senior Facilitators so that every team has a consistent support contact.

The employee runs short, structured mentoring conversations each week. These conversations check module progress, workshop preparation, research briefs, reflections, team dynamics, questions about community facilitation, and the participant’s confidence in the upcoming task. The employee uses coaching questions rather than simply supplying answers, while escalating factual, safety, academic, accessibility, or personal concerns through the approved process.

The employee helps teams use feedback from older adult participants, site observers, peers, and Senior Facilitators. They teach participants to identify one strength, one improvement priority, and one concrete change for the next session. Where a team is struggling, the employee coordinates a rehearsal, peer observation, or additional support session with the Learning and Curriculum Lead.

The employee notices and addresses barriers to participation, such as confusion about roles, missed deadlines, unclear group communication, or anxiety about facilitating. They maintain respectful boundaries and refer concerns outside their role to the Program Coordinator / Academic Lead or an appropriate university support channel.

Success in this role is demonstrated when participant teams are prepared, supported, responsive to feedback, and able to work collaboratively and respectfully with older adults and peers.

## Qualifications and requirements

The successful candidate is a currently enrolled Trent University student, subject to the employment requirements in effect at appointment. They demonstrate empathy, active listening, reliable follow-through, clear communication, and sound boundaries.

The candidate can support peers without making assumptions, can de-escalate routine group misunderstandings, and can recognize when to involve a supervisor. Experience in mentoring, residence life, peer support, tutoring, student leadership, community work, or group facilitation is an asset.

The candidate must complete required training on mentoring boundaries, confidentiality, accessibility, anti-oppression and respectful conduct, and escalation procedures.

## Reporting relationships

The Student Mentorship and Team Support Lead reports to the Program Coordinator / Academic Lead. The role works with the Assessment and Feedback Lead to identify participants who may need support and with the Learning and Curriculum Lead to arrange additional practice. It does not provide counselling, academic accommodation decisions, or final assessment decisions.

# Position 5 — Senior Facilitator, Digital Safety and Current-Threat Lead

## Position summary

The Senior Facilitator, Digital Safety and Current-Threat Lead keeps the program’s cybersecurity examples accurate, credible, relevant, and suitable for community education. This employee supports participants to recognize phishing, impersonation, financial fraud, identity theft, account compromise, suspicious links, social engineering, misinformation, and emerging AI-enabled risks, then communicate safe next steps without alarmism.

## Key responsibilities and duties

The Digital Safety and Current-Threat Lead develops a weekly current-threat briefing process. They help participants locate credible information, distinguish evidence from rumours, assess whether an issue is relevant to older adults, and translate findings into clear, non-alarming guidance. They support the planned four short current-threat or emerging-technology briefs and coordinate the proposed feedback schedule with the Assessment and Feedback Lead.

The employee reviews workshop scenarios, slide examples, handouts, and demonstrations for factual accuracy and safe advice. They make sure that examples do not normalize risky actions, such as clicking suspicious links, sharing passwords, installing unverified software, or giving remote access to an unknown person. They support the Learning and Curriculum Lead in teaching passphrases, password managers, multi-factor authentication, privacy settings, safe browsing, software updates, and account/device security.

The employee models appropriate responses when a participant or older adult brings a suspicious message to a workshop. They explain that the facilitator’s role is to slow down, avoid clicking or responding, preserve relevant information where appropriate, and guide the person toward approved reporting or support options. They do not diagnose fraud, access a person’s account, contact a bank on a person’s behalf, or request sensitive credentials.

The employee maintains a current reference list of approved public sources, with the Program Coordinator / Academic Lead, and shares concise updates with the facilitation team. They flag urgent content changes or emerging risks that may require a revised scenario or an approved advisory to participants.

Success in this role is demonstrated when program content remains current and accurate, participant threat briefs are evidence-based, and community guidance is practical, calm, privacy-respecting, and safe.

## Qualifications and requirements

The successful candidate is a currently enrolled Trent University student, subject to the employment requirements in effect at appointment. They have demonstrated interest, coursework, experience, or strong self-directed learning in cybersecurity, digital privacy, information evaluation, artificial intelligence, digital literacy, or a related field.

The candidate can assess source credibility, summarize a risk clearly, and communicate uncertainty honestly. They understand that cybersecurity education for the public must be accurate and non-alarming. Research, technical-support, library, communications, or digital-literacy experience is an asset.

The candidate must complete required training on privacy, safe facilitation boundaries, approved public guidance, and escalation. They must seek supervisor review before distributing material framed as official financial, legal, technical, or incident-response advice.

## Reporting relationships

The Digital Safety and Current-Threat Lead reports to the Program Coordinator / Academic Lead. The role works closely with the Learning and Curriculum Lead and the Assessment and Feedback Lead. It provides content advice to the full facilitation team but does not replace approved academic or university subject-matter review.

# Position 6 — Senior Facilitator, Accessibility, Technology and Resources Lead

## Position summary

The Senior Facilitator, Accessibility, Technology and Resources Lead ensures that the program’s learning environment, workshop materials, digital demonstrations, and participant resources are usable, prepared, and age-friendly. This employee supports inclusive delivery by making technical steps visible, paced, repeatable, and adaptable to different digital-confidence levels.

## Key responsibilities and duties

The Accessibility, Technology and Resources Lead prepares the equipment and materials needed for learning sessions and community workshops. This includes confirming devices, displays, internet access, chargers, presentation files, printed or digital handouts, large-print or accessible-format materials where approved, and backup demonstration plans if connectivity fails.

The employee reviews teaching materials with the Learning and Curriculum Lead to ensure that instructions are legible, logically sequenced, and written in plain language. They encourage use of short steps, screenshots or demonstrations where appropriate, sufficient white space, clear headings, time for hands-on practice, and multiple opportunities to ask questions. They do not assume that every older adult participant owns the same device or uses the same operating system.

The employee teaches program participants how to facilitate basic video-conferencing and digital-communication tasks safely. This includes joining a call, using mute and camera controls, recognizing suspicious invitations, applying basic privacy controls, and asking permission before changing a person’s device setting. The employee also supports introductory AI demonstrations that emphasize privacy, limitations, verification, and safe prompting.

The employee maintains the approved resource inventory and version control for workshop plans, handouts, templates, observation forms, scenario cards, and reference links. They coordinate with the Community Workshop Operations Lead on room set-up and with the Digital Safety and Current-Threat Lead when a resource requires factual updating.

Success in this role is demonstrated when sessions can proceed despite routine technology changes, participants have accessible and usable materials, and older adult attendees can follow a demonstration without being rushed or excluded.

## Qualifications and requirements

The successful candidate is a currently enrolled Trent University student, subject to employment requirements in effect at appointment. They demonstrate strong practical digital skills, careful preparation, patience, and an ability to troubleshoot routine technology issues without taking control away from the learner.

The candidate understands the value of accessibility, plain language, clear formatting, and hands-on practice. Experience providing technical support, creating accessible documents, supporting workshops, working in a library or community program, or tutoring is an asset.

The candidate must complete required training on accessibility, privacy, community-facilitation boundaries, approved use of devices and software, and site-specific procedures.

## Reporting relationships

The Accessibility, Technology and Resources Lead reports to the Program Coordinator / Academic Lead. The role works closely with the Community Workshop Operations Lead and Learning and Curriculum Lead. It may recommend accessible delivery changes, but accommodation decisions and commitments remain with the appropriate university authority.

## Recommended coverage map

The six specialist portfolios should be assigned at the start of the program, but every Senior Facilitator should be able to cover a core cohort-support and workshop-observation function. The following operating map prevents the program from depending on one employee for a critical activity.

| Program function | Primary role | Required supporting role |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly curriculum, demonstrations, and practice activities | Learning and Curriculum Lead | Digital Safety and Current-Threat Lead; Accessibility, Technology and Resources Lead |
| Partner schedules, room readiness, and workshop staffing | Community Workshop Operations Lead | Accessibility, Technology and Resources Lead |
| Evidence tracker, feedback cycle, and missing-evidence alerts | Assessment and Feedback Lead | Student Mentorship and Team Support Lead |
| Participant check-ins, team coaching, and early support | Student Mentorship and Team Support Lead | All Senior Facilitators |
| Current-threat briefs, risk scenarios, and cybersecurity accuracy | Digital Safety and Current-Threat Lead | Learning and Curriculum Lead |
| Materials, device readiness, accessible delivery, and backups | Accessibility, Technology and Resources Lead | Community Workshop Operations Lead |
| Live workshop observation and debrief | Assigned Senior Facilitator observer | Assessment and Feedback Lead; Community Workshop Operations Lead |

Final employment classification, wage rate, work hours, reporting title, screening requirements, accommodation process, community-partner responsibilities, assessment rubric, and hiring language must be confirmed through Trent University’s normal approval and human-resources processes before recruitment.
